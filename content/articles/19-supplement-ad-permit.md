*Written by the Neoi - Health Supplements Singapore editorial team - Last updated: 28 July 2026*

A supplement ad permit is not required for every health supplement advertisement in Singapore. HSA says advertisements and promotions for health supplements are generally not subject to permit approval, except when a product is classified as a quasi-medicinal product. Classification and claim compliance are separate checks. This guide is educational information, not medical or legal advice, and it does not classify a specific product.

## Separate permit approval from claim compliance

“No permit approval” does not mean “no advertising rules.” HSA's Health Supplements Guidelines say claims can appear directly or indirectly on labels, advertisements, point-of-sale materials, and brochures. Claims must fit the health-supplement category and be supported by relevant evidence.

HSA's claim guidance also says product materials must not mislead through ambiguity, exaggeration, or omission. Phrases such as “clinically proven” should be avoided for health supplements, and advertisements must also follow the Singapore Code of Advertising Practice where applicable. A permit question therefore does not replace a claim review.

The QM flowchart is specifically for health supplements containing vitamins or minerals. Its branches consider more than the presence of a vitamin: they also ask about the product's label, health claims, active ingredients, and the proportion of vitamin or mineral active ingredients. Use the live flowchart rather than a simplified rule copied from a seller.

## Build an eight-field ad-route record

Original element 2, captured on 28 July 2026: this record translates the official tool inputs and HSA [claims-assessment guidance](https://file.go.gov.sg/hsa-chp-claims-guidelines.pdf) into one reusable checklist.

1. **Product presentation:** record the dosage form, pack front, and intended use.
2. **Active ingredients:** copy every active ingredient and quantity without grouping unlike ingredients.
3. **Vitamin or mineral source:** retain the source material named for each relevant nutrient.
4. **Label category:** copy whether the pack calls itself a vitamin, mineral, health supplement, food, or another product type.
5. **Exact claim:** save the full wording, including headline, qualifiers, image text, and nearby disclaimers.
6. **Proposed channel:** note whether the material is a label, web listing, social post, brochure, point-of-sale display, or public promotion.
7. **Official route evidence:** save the current flowchart or tool outcome and any HSA classification advice obtained.
8. **Version record:** keep the source URLs and the date checked, because guidance can change.

Do not fill missing ingredient or classification facts from memory. The CHP tool tells users to check with the manufacturer or supplier when required product information is unavailable.

## Know what the route does not prove

A permit, where required, is not a general promise that a product is effective, suitable for an individual, or endorsed by HSA. Conversely, an advertisement that follows a no-permit route is not automatically acceptable. Claim wording, evidence, product presentation, and public-facing context still matter.

The HSA [claims substantiation guideline](https://file.go.gov.sg/hsa-chp-claims-guidelines.pdf) says evidence must be relevant to the claim. It also warns against partial or out-of-context scientific quotations and against wording that implies greater validity than a product can provide. Keep the exact product, exact claim, and supporting evidence connected in the same record.

## Stop when classification is unclear

Use HSA's CHP Classification Tool when the route is uncertain. It asks for ingredient names and quantities, botanical details where relevant, vitamin and mineral sources, intended use, and dosage form. If the available facts do not support a clear route, pause the advertisement and use the official classification-enquiry path rather than choosing the easier answer.

For a reader evaluating an existing advertisement, the same pause is useful: do not treat missing permit information as proof of a breach, and do not treat an advertised permit number as proof of safety or suitability. Check the product category and current official source before drawing a conclusion.

## FAQ

**Does every Singapore supplement advertisement need an HSA permit?** No. HSA says health-supplement advertisements are generally not subject to permit approval, except for products classified as quasi-medicinal products.

**Do all products containing vitamins or minerals need an ad permit?** Do not use that shortcut. The official QM flowchart considers several product facts and directs uncertain cases to the CHP tool or a classification enquiry.

**Can an advertisement skip claim checks when no permit is required?** No. HSA's health-supplement claim principles still apply, including truthfulness, relevant substantiation, suitable category wording, and avoidance of misleading messages.

**Does a permit mean HSA endorses the supplement?** Do not treat a permit as an endorsement, efficacy finding, authenticity finding, or personal suitability assessment.
